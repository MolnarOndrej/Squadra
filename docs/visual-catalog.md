# Visual catalog

Every look Squadra has, by type: what it is called, what makes it recognizable, how it renders, and the Unity assets that define it. Browse it before planning new visuals; the workflow for adding looks is in [the visual variant workflow](../specifications/visual-variant-workflow-spec.md).

The pictures are rendered by the game, from the Sandbox scene with the game's camera, space backdrop and post-processing, by [`render-pictures.cs`](visual-catalog/render-pictures.cs) (see [Adding a look](#adding-a-look)). Each shows the look alone:

- **Worlds**: a body of radius 1 in a view 3 units high, widened where needed to fit planetary rings. Ownership indicators, auras and labels are hidden.
- **Scene backgrounds**: the whole Sandbox battlefield framing in a 16:9 view, with nothing in front.
- **Darts**: one Blue Dart, nose to the right, at the size and tint the game draws it with, in a view twice its length high.

The descriptions also say what still shows at gameplay scale: the default size of the type (planet 1, moon 0.5, asteroid 0.3) at the scale the Sandbox battlefield gets in a Game view 1080 pixels high. A world given another size in a mission, or a larger map, shows more or less of it.

## Planets

World surfaces are listed in [`WorldLooks.asset`](../Assets/Squadra/Visuals/WorldLooks.asset). Ember, Thalassa, Lune, Cinder and Ferrite use [`Squadra/Planet`](../Assets/Squadra/Visuals/Shaders/Planet.shader). The eight other planets use [`Squadra/Mapped Planet`](../Assets/Squadra/Visuals/Shaders/MappedPlanet.shader) with 4096 × 2048 mipmapped surface maps. The ten planet materials each set their own spin speed. Pelagos, Virelia and Vesper carry drifting clouds that shade the layers beneath them. Halite, Rime, Sahra and Solfatara use the alpha channel for relief so basin edges and crater rims catch light during rotation. Dust in Aureole and Rime's rings turns with the globe at a fixed tilt. The rings and globe shade each other from a fixed upper-left light direction. The globe hides the far arc and gameplay indicators are drawn over both.

## Asteroids

Asteroid surfaces have no texture. Cinder and Ferrite use [`Squadra/Planet`](../Assets/Squadra/Visuals/Shaders/Planet.shader): a lit ball whose outline dips in and out (`_Lumps`), with procedural craters and colors. The five other asteroids use [`Squadra/Asteroid`](../Assets/Squadra/Visuals/Shaders/Asteroid.shader), which sphere-traces a small 3D rock inside the same quad. Its `Form` keyword picks the rock and its large feature, so blades, fissures, lobes, facets and ledges are real shape: their outline changes as they turn and they shade and shadow themselves under the battlefield's light. Rocks with a feature that must stay in view sway about their spin axis instead of turning all the way. The dust plume and the stones are drawn by the same material in the quad's margin around the body (a smaller `_BodyRadius` makes room), so they belong to the look; the world's body radius, picking and indicators are unchanged, and the indicators are drawn over them. The catalog pictures fix the effect time at 0.

## Adding a look

Add or update entries in the same change that adds the look to the game: one entry per look in its type's section, and a new section for a new type. Looks still being worked on stay out of the catalog.

Render the pictures with [`render-pictures.cs`](visual-catalog/render-pictures.cs), so new pictures match the ones here. With the editor open, out of Play Mode and with every scene saved, run from the project folder:

```powershell
unity command script eval --project-path . -- --file docs/visual-catalog/render-pictures.cs
```

- It renders every look of the game into `docs/visual-catalog/<type>-<id>.png` and lists the files it wrote: the surfaces and backdrops in `WorldLooks.asset` (**Squadra > Rebuild Mission Scene** lists the materials put in `Materials/Planets`, `Moons`, `Asteroids` and `Backdrops`), and every material in `Materials/Darts`, drawn with the mesh of the same name in `Visuals/Meshes`, or else the one in `FleetStyle.asset`. A Dart's ID is its material's file name in lower case. The mapped planets show their starting longitude. The older globes turn and the nebula drifts between runs, so commit only the pictures of the looks you added or changed.
- With `comparison = true` it also writes one strip per type to `Recordings/VisualCatalog/compare-<type>.png` (not committed): every look of the type side by side at gameplay scale, and for Darts a Blue, a Red and a Green one per look. Looks still being worked on go in its `candidates` list, by asset path, under any folder named `Planets`, `Moons`, `Asteroids`, `Backdrops` or `Darts` (a subfolder such as `Materials/Planets/Work` keeps them out of the game); they appear in the strips, with their close-ups beside them, and never in `docs`. Nothing is assigned or saved. Use it to judge new looks against their closest existing look and each other, then check them in the Sandbox Game view next to rings, labels, routes and fleets.
- A new type is not covered: render it with a temporary preview of its own, at the scale it will have on screen.
